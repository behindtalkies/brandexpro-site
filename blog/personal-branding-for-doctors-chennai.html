Personal Branding for Doctors in Chennai: What Works Under the New NMC Rules

Brandex Studio, Valasaravakkam13 October 20267 min read

Personal branding for doctors in Chennai in 2026 means consistent educational video under your own name, with your qualifications and registration number disclosed, and no patient testimonials, no before-and-after clips and no guaranteed outcomes. The National Medical Commission's guidelines of 6 October 2026 closed the shortcuts. What's left is the slow, durable route: being the doctor patients already recognise and trust before they ever walk into your clinic.

Patients in Chennai look a doctor up before they book. They search the name, open Instagram, watch whatever appears on YouTube. If nothing appears, the search ends at a hospital listing or a review site, and someone else's page decides how you're perceived. Personal branding is simply taking that page back.

What changed on 6 October 2026

The NMC's Ethics and Medical Registration Board issued advertising guidelines that apply immediately to every registered practitioner. Reported coverage of the guidelines sets out the lines clearly:

  • No patient testimonials requested or shared for promotion, including through an agency or influencer.
  • No before-and-after depictions, even with consent.
  • No guaranteed outcomes and no claims of superiority over other doctors.
  • Disclosure on every post: name, qualifications, registration status and State or National Medical Register number.
  • Educational and awareness content is allowed, as long as it informs rather than solicits.
  • Paid posts, sponsored content, influencer promotion and AI-generated promotional material all count as advertising, and routing them through a third party does not shift responsibility.

Penalties can reach suspension or removal from the register. So the first thing a doctor in Chennai should ask any studio or agency is: do you know these rules, and will you keep me inside them? If the answer is a blank look, keep looking.

Strip out the prohibited formats and what remains is, frankly, the content patients wanted all along. The formats that work:

Tamil, English, or both?

This is the question Chennai doctors ask us most. The answer depends on who you treat. A nephrologist at a tertiary hospital drawing patients from across Tamil Nadu reaches more people in Tamil. A dermatologist in Nungambakkam with a young, professional patient base may do better in English or Tanglish. Many of our doctors film the same script twice, once in each language, on the same shoot day. Captions in both scripts widen reach further and are close to mandatory for mobile viewers watching on mute.

How much time does this take from a working doctor?

Less than most expect, if it's organised as a batch. A typical month at Brandex works like this:

  1. Script call (30–45 minutes). We go through the questions patients actually ask you, pick 10–15 topics, and write short, spoken-language scripts you approve on WhatsApp.
  2. One shoot morning (about 3 hours). You come to the studio in Valasaravakkam, or we set up at your clinic. Cinema cameras, proper lighting, a teleprompter if you want one. We film the talking-head pieces plus B-roll: you with a stethoscope, reviewing a scan, walking into the consult room.
  3. We edit and you approve. Cuts, captions, lower-thirds with your name, qualification and register number, music, thumbnails. You review on your phone.
  4. We publish on a schedule. YouTube, Instagram and Facebook, spaced across the month, so you are visible every week without touching an app.

One morning produces a month of content. That cadence is what builds recognition; a single viral video rarely does.

The disclosure habit

Put your name, degree and registration number in a lower-third on every video and in the first line of every caption. It satisfies the NMC requirement, and it quietly does your branding for you: patients remember the credentials they see fifty times.

Why B-roll matters more for doctors than anyone else

A doctor talking to camera for 60 seconds straight looks like a lecture. The same doctor, with four-second cutaways of the clinic, the equipment, the team, a patient-free consult room, looks like someone you already know. That footage is what we call B-roll, and it is the single biggest difference between a phone video and a studio-produced one. It also keeps you compliant: B-roll of your facilities and equipment is factual information the guidelines permit, while patient footage is a minefield.

What to check before you hire anyone in Chennai

  • Ask to see videos they've made for other doctors, and look for disclosures, not just production quality.
  • Ask who writes scripts and whether they'll flag a claim that crosses the line.
  • Ask how many videos one shoot day produces, and how publishing is handled.
  • Ask whether editing is in-house. Outsourced editing is where medical nuance gets lost.

Frequently asked questions

Can doctors in India post videos on Instagram and YouTube?

Yes. Educational and health-awareness content is permitted under the NMC guidelines issued on 6 October 2026, provided it informs rather than solicits patients and every post discloses the doctor's name, qualifications and registration number.

Can a doctor use patient testimonials for personal branding?

No. The October 2026 NMC guidelines prohibit requesting or sharing patient testimonials for promotion, including through agencies or influencers. Before-and-after depictions are also prohibited, even with consent.

What kind of videos should a doctor make?

Myth-versus-fact explainers, how to read common reports, when to see a specialist, what to expect from a procedure, seasonal health awareness and behind-the-practice footage of your clinic and team. All are educational and compliant.

Should Chennai doctors make videos in Tamil or English?

It depends on your patient base. Doctors serving patients from across Tamil Nadu usually reach more people in Tamil; those with a young, urban base often use English or a mix. Many film both versions on the same shoot day.

How much time does personal branding take for a working doctor?

With a batch system, roughly one 30–45 minute script call and one 3-hour shoot morning per month. The studio handles editing, captions, disclosures and publishing across platforms.

Does personal branding help a doctor get more patients?

It builds recognition and trust before the first visit, which is what patients act on when choosing between doctors. Under NMC rules it cannot promise outcomes or solicit, so think of it as reputation, not advertising.
